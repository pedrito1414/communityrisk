# Scottish local–national link review — 4 November 2026

This is an editorial classification for browsing, not approval by the register publishers. The 26 local topics have 50 links: 2 direct and 48 broader. All ratings remain unchanged and unscored. Existing England/Wales classifications have not been reclassified.

Direct means the local text describes the same national scenario (national transmission-system blackout). Broader means a local topic includes or provides context for a narrower national scenario, without confirming the national scale, geography, thresholds or assumptions. For cyber and transport, links identify narrower examples within the broad topic, not separately assessed local scenarios.

## Decisions and exclusions

- Flooding links to coastal and fluvial scenarios. Flash flooding is not sufficient evidence of drainage-overload surface-water flooding, so no surface-water link was added. The national coastal worst case is on the east coast of England: Scottish links are thematic only.
- Severe weather includes storm-force winds, extreme temperatures and snow/cold. Links are broad; national thresholds are not imputed locally.
- Animal/plant health links only to the named foot and mouth and avian influenza topics. No named plant-pest, African swine fever or African horse sickness link was inferred.
- Industrial accidents link to onshore major-hazard fire/explosion and toxic release as narrower examples. No nuclear, offshore or specific pipeline accident was inferred solely from a general industrial heading.
- Pollution links to maritime pollution described locally. Food or water impacts alone do not establish the national food-contamination or water-infrastructure failure scenarios.
- General public-service cyber attacks link to health/social care and police as narrower examples of public services. These links are broad, with explicit caveats; infrastructure-sector attacks are not inferred from downstream effects.
- Transport links to rail, passenger-vessel and aviation accidents as narrower examples of accidents across the named modes. This does not establish the national accident scale; weather and fuel causes remain context, rather than extra transport-scenario matches.
